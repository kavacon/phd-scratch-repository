\section{System assessments}\label{sec:system_assessments}
The instrument defined in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation_instrument} is applied to eleven primary systems using tasks T1--T4 and conditions C1--C9.
The extraction dataset and assessed code are published in Appendices~C and~D of~\cite{kavadias2026qplSurveySupplementary}.
The summaries below retain discriminating evidence and task-specific qualifications; Table~\ref{tab:system_assessments:system_summary} reports the aggregated profiles.
Tower remains excluded from primary assessment.
Interpretation of RQ1 and RQ2 is reserved for Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{Per-system evidence summaries}\label{subsec:system_assessments:per_system}

\subsubsection{OpenQASM 3.0}\label{subsubsec:system_assessments:per_system:openqasm}
The representative constructions use named gates on explicit, indexed \texttt{qubit} registers, with \texttt{measure} operations extracting classical results in T1--T3~\cite{cross2022openqasm3,kavadias2026qplSurveySupplementary}.
T1 and T2 are constructed examples.
The recorded profile assigns OpenQASM~3.0 to Assembly, with C2, C3, C6 and C9 all F.
The C4 judgement records explicit reversal in T1--T3: the second Hadamard layer, the diffusion sequence \texttt{h; x; cz; x; h} and \texttt{inv @ phase(c)}.
C9 is constrained in T1 and T3 through user-defined gates and modifiers, but fails in the inlined T2 and T4 constructions.
C8 is constrained in T1--T3 and fails in the Bell example.
C7 passes throughout because the examples use standard gates without exercising timing or pulse-level calibration, differing from the expected Assembly profile.

\subsubsection{QIR}\label{subsubsec:system_assessments:per_system:qir}
The assessed examples are LLVM-IR function bodies containing quantum intrinsic calls over opaque qubit and result handles; T1--T3 are constructed fragments~\cite{qir_alliance_qir_spec_repo,kavadias2026qplSurveySupplementary}.
QIR is assigned to IR, with C2, C3 and C9 failing throughout.
C4 records F for the explicit reversal steps in T1--T3, including the second Hadamard layer, diffusion and inverse-QFT decomposition.
Although LLVM provides \texttt{define} and \texttt{call}, the assessed fragments contain flat intrinsic sequences, supporting the recorded C9 failure.
C8 is constrained in T1--T2 and fails in T3--T4.
C6 is unassessable because the evidence does not exercise result extraction.
This is an evidence-coverage limitation, not a pass or a claim that QIR lacks measurement capability.
C7 passes in the representative suite.

\subsubsection{Q-MLIR}\label{subsubsec:system_assessments:per_system:qmlir}
The assessed surface is a qcor-style \texttt{\_\_qpu\_\_} kernel using \texttt{qreg}, \texttt{qalloc(N)} and gate calls on indexed qubits~\cite{McCaskey2021MLIRDialectQASM,kavadias2026qplSurveySupplementary}.
T1, T3 and T4 are constructed examples.
Q-MLIR is assigned to IR, with C1, C2, C3 and C5 failing throughout.
C4 fails in T1 and T3 but is constrained in T2, where \texttt{compute \{...\} action \{...\}} provides structured reversal.
Kernel definitions and calls support constrained C9 outcomes rather than value-level composition.
C8 is constrained in T1 and fails in T2--T4.
The abstract gate namespace permits C7 to pass without removing the explicit qubit and gate model.

\subsubsection{Qiskit}\label{subsubsec:system_assessments:per_system:qiskit}
Each construction authors and mutates a \texttt{QuantumCircuit} through named gate calls and explicit qubit identities~\cite{aleksandrowicz2019qiskit,kavadias2026qplSurveySupplementary}.
Even the minimal Bell example requires a circuit object and indexed Hadamard and controlled-X operations.
The recorded profile assigns Qiskit to Framework.
Composition through \texttt{qc.compose}, Grover operators and \texttt{QFT(3, inverse=True)} operates on circuits rather than replacing circuit authoring.
C9 therefore records C in the aggregate.
Explicit quantum/classical registers and measurement placement retain failed C5 and C6 outcomes.
C8 is constrained in T1--T3 but fails in T4, while C4 remains F and C7 P in the aggregated profile.

\subsubsection{PennyLane}\label{subsubsec:system_assessments:per_system:pennylane}
Named operations on identified wires inside a device-bound \texttt{@qml.qnode} retain an explicit circuit authoring model~\cite{bergholm2018pennylane,kavadias2026qplSurveySupplementary}.
PennyLane is assigned to Framework.
T1 and T2 follow preparation, oracle, interference and readout recipes.
T3 delegates phase-estimation structure to \texttt{qml.QuantumPhaseEstimation}, giving a task-local constrained C4 outcome without changing the circuit artefact.
C4 remains F in the aggregate.
Explicit staging and classical post-processing retain C5=F.
Bell preparation contributes C8=F, while circuit-level reuse supports C9=C.
Observation is unassessable in T3 and T4 but fails in T1 and T2, producing the recorded aggregate C6=F.

\subsubsection{Q\#}\label{subsubsec:system_assessments:per_system:qsharp}
The representative programs allocate qubits and apply typed operations using \texttt{use}, \texttt{within \{...\} apply \{...\}}, \texttt{Adjoint} and \texttt{Controlled}~\cite{MicrosoftQSharpOverview,kavadias2026qplSurveySupplementary}.
T3 is constructed.
The recorded profile assigns Q\# to Framework despite its standalone language surface.
Structured reversal avoids manually duplicating inverse gate sequences, yielding C4=C across the main algorithmic tasks.
Operation-level reuse supports C9=C and hardware concerns remain optional, giving C7=P.
Explicit qubit commitments, quantum/classical boundaries and result extraction retain failures on C1--C3, C5 and C6.
Phase estimation and Bell preparation require phase or entanglement reasoning, producing aggregate C8=F.

\subsubsection{Silq}\label{subsubsec:system_assessments:per_system:silq}
The assessed Silq programs manipulate quantum-typed values through functions rather than assembling circuit objects~\cite{bichsel2020silq,kavadias2026qplSurveySupplementary}.
T3 and T4 are constructed examples.
Reusable oracle and diffusion routines, together with a \texttt{bell\_pair} routine whose result is consumed by another routine, support C9=P.
Result extraction remains explicit through \texttt{measure}, but without classical-register wiring, giving C6=C.
The recorded profile assigns Silq to High-level, with C2--C6 constrained and C7 passing.
Addressed Hadamard applications retain C1=F.
The constructed phase-estimation example requires explicit phase reasoning and inverse-QFT decomposition, driving C8=F despite the program-level organisation.

\subsubsection{Qmod (Classiq)}\label{subsubsec:system_assessments:per_system:qmod}
Typed \texttt{qfunc}/\texttt{qperm} routines, explicit allocation and callable parameters provide reusable quantum program structure~\cite{vax2025qmodexpressivehighlevelquantum,kavadias2026qplSurveySupplementary}.
T1 uses \texttt{within\_apply}; T2 uses \texttt{power} and \texttt{grover\_operator}.
The recorded profile assigns Qmod to High-level, with C9=P and C2, C3, C5 and C6 constrained.
T3 is constructed and retains explicit controlled powers, inverse-QFT code and \texttt{measure(count)}.
C4 is recorded as failed because this phase-estimation construction requires manual inverse-QFT decomposition as ordinary code.
Addressed Hadamard applications drive aggregate C1=F, and explicit phase reasoning in T3 drives C8=F.
Hardware adjacency remains optional, giving C7=P.

\subsubsection{Quff}\label{subsubsec:system_assessments:per_system:quff}
Ordinary functions and higher-order oracle use coexist with quantum blocks, indexed registers and explicit \texttt{measure} calls~\cite{wright2024quff,kavadias2026qplSurveySupplementary}.
T1, T3 and T4 are constructed examples.
The recorded profile assigns Quff to Framework.
Program-first organisation supports C2=C and C9=P, while explicit register identity retains C3=F.
Inversion remains visible through \texttt{invertF(f, q)} in Grover and \texttt{invertF(qft, count)} in phase estimation, supporting C4=C.
Compact result extraction supports C6=C rather than eliminating observation.
The constructed phase-estimation unitary and controlled-power pattern retain explicit phase reasoning, producing C8=F.
C1 also fails because superposition begins through quantum gate idioms.

\subsubsection{Qutes}\label{subsubsec:system_assessments:per_system:qutes}
The task suite spans classical-looking substring membership and explicitly quantum programs~\cite{faro2025qutes,reversiblelifetime2026,kavadias2026qplSurveySupplementary}.
T3 and T4 are constructed.
T2 expresses search as \texttt{"01" in b}, without visible qubits, circuit objects, measurement calls or amplitude reasoning; all nine conditions pass for that task.
Other tasks retain quantum types, initialisation, indexed resources and gate operations.
Result extraction through classical assignment or \texttt{measure} supports aggregate C6=C.
The recorded profile assigns Qutes to Framework, with C2, C4--C6 constrained and C9 passing.
Task-local relief in T2 does not remove aggregate C1 and C3 failures, while the constructed phase-estimation example drives C8=F.

\subsubsection{Qwerty}\label{subsubsec:system_assessments:per_system:qwerty}
Basis translations and oracle lifting express program structure without circuit-object assembly~\cite{adams2024qwerty,kavadias2026qplSurveySupplementary}.
T1 and T2 use \texttt{oracle.sign} and translations such as \texttt{pm**N >> std**N}.
These tasks pass C2, C3 and C4, while explicit \texttt{@qpu} staging and observation remain visible.
T4 introduces a Bell pair through \texttt{'00' + '11'} and consumes it through another routine.
T3 retains controlled powers, \texttt{fourier[prec].measure} and phase or eigenvalue reasoning.
The aggregate profile assigns Qwerty to Framework: C1--C4 are constrained, C5, C6 and C8 fail, and C7 and C9 pass.
Qwerty is the only reviewed system with aggregate C1=C rather than F.

\subsection{Comparative analysis}\label{subsec:system_assessments:comparative}
Tier groupings refer to the assignments in Table~\ref{tab:system_assessments:system_summary}.

\subsubsection{Condition patterns and constraint drivers}\label{subsubsec:system_assessments:condition_patterns}
C7 passes across all eleven aggregated profiles.
The representative constructions therefore do not require hardware-specific authoring obligations.
This does not imply that the systems lack hardware-control interfaces: the OpenQASM examples, for instance, do not exercise its timing and calibration extensions.

C2 and C3 remain persistent constraints.
Circuit and IR constructions expose circuit objects, gate sequences or intrinsic calls alongside registers, wires or handles.
Program-oriented systems improve C2 to constrained outcomes and often pass C9, but these improvements do not eliminate qubit identity across the suite.
Composition and resource abstraction consequently improve independently rather than together.

C6 also distinguishes compact observation from absent observation.
Silq, Qmod, Quff and Qutes compress extraction into program-level operations and record constrained aggregate outcomes.
Qwerty retains explicit observation despite task-local improvements in artefact and identity.
QIR's unassessable C6 records incomplete observation evidence, not a positive result.

Task-specific profiles qualify these aggregates.
Qutes passes every condition in the membership-test task but retains quantum-explicit obligations elsewhere.
Qwerty passes C2 and C3 in Deutsch--Jozsa and Grover, yet its phase-estimation construction retains constrained outcomes.
Its aggregate C1=C distinguishes basis-oriented initiation from the gate-idiom initiation recorded for the remaining systems.

Required-knowledge outcomes also depend on the task.
Phase estimation concentrates failures involving phase or eigenvalue reasoning, while explicit Bell construction contributes entanglement-related failures in several circuit-oriented examples.
Under the recorded aggregation, favourable task-local outcomes do not remove C8 failure elsewhere in the suite.
The profiles therefore show selective relief of programming obligations, not uniform improvement across every condition.

\subsubsection{Quantum-implicit gap finding}\label{subsubsec:system_assessments:qi_gap}
No reviewed system satisfies the complete quantum-implicit gate set under the recorded task evidence.
Classification requires C2, C3, C6 and C9 to pass, together with a justified match to the tier definition.
Each aggregated profile lacks at least one required pass.

Circuit- and IR-centred constructions retain explicit artefacts and qubit identities.
Program-oriented constructions improve organisation, composition and sometimes observation handling, but retain quantum-specific obligations elsewhere.
Qutes's membership operation and Qwerty's task-local artefact and identity passes demonstrate that individual workflows can relieve several obligations without satisfying the full profile across the suite.

The quantum-implicit tier is therefore unoccupied within these eleven systems and representative constructions.
The finding is bounded by the included systems, task suite and evidence base; its implications for RQ1 and RQ2 are considered in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.
