\section{Review methodology}\label{sec:methodology}
The review follows the evidence-based software engineering guidelines~\cite{KitchenhamCharters2007SLR}, with a pre-registered protocol available at~\cite{kavadias2026qplSurveyProtocol}.
The evaluation instrument is defined in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation_instrument}; results are reported in Section~\ref{sec:system_assessments}.

\subsection{Search protocol}\label{subsec:methodology:search_protocol}
Searches were conducted on 2026-05-19 across ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore and arXiv.
Official project and specification sites were consulted per system throughout the assessment period.
The literature cutoff was 2026-04-08; later evidence was excluded from assessment.

Three verbatim search strings were applied consistently across all databases:
\begin{description}
    \item[S1: broad seed terms] \texttt{"quantum programming language" OR "quantum intermediate representation" OR OpenQASM OR "quantum MLIR" OR Silq OR Qiskit OR PennyLane}.
    \item[S2: system-specific terms] \texttt{Silq quantum OR Quff quantum OR Qutes quantum OR Quipper quantum OR "Microsoft Q\#" OR "Q-MLIR"}.
    \item[S3: abstraction framing] \texttt{"quantum programming" AND "abstraction" AND "language"}.
\end{description}
Full search strings, per-database result counts and search URLs are published in Appendix~A of~\cite{kavadias2026qplSurveySupplementary}.
The searches retrieved 852 records from arXiv, 1{,}295 from ACM Digital Library and 704 from IEEE Xplore.
The combined 2{,}851 records yielded 2{,}233 unique records after deduplication.

Supplementary AI-assisted discovery used a scheduled agent to scan active project context and surface candidate papers fortnightly.
Its dynamically generated queries are not reproducible verbatim, so it does not constitute a primary PRISMA search~\cite{page2021prisma}.
All surfaced candidates were screened against the pre-specified criteria before inclusion.

\subsection{Screening and selection}\label{subsec:methodology:screening}
A pre-specified, bounded seed set of quantum programming systems informed search-strategy design and validation.
Database searches provided the primary mechanism for discovering additional candidates and reducing discovery bias.
Extraction and assessment concern each included system's authoritative evidence base, comprising primary papers or specifications and official documentation, rather than the full retrieved corpus.

A system is included if it meets at least three of four criteria:
\begin{description}
    \item[$C_1$] A formal specification or peer-reviewed primary paper exists.
    \item[$C_2$] Active releases or specification updates within 24 months prior to the cutoff.
    \item[$C_3$] Multivendor or multi-device backend support beyond a single lab or vendor.
    \item[$C_4$] Public documentation is enough to complete the representative tasks defined in Section~\ref{subsec:evaluation_instrument:task_suite} without access to private knowledge.
\end{description}
Exclusions comprise marketing-only primary materials, projects inactive for more than 36 months and documentation insufficient for representative workflows.
The evaluation target is the latest stable major release or specification available by 2026-04-08.
Earlier versions are used only as labelled historical contrasts.

Screening was conducted by a single reviewer.
The review covers a pre-defined system set rather than a large open literature corpus.
Per-system full-text exclusion rationales are published in Appendix~B of~\cite{kavadias2026qplSurveySupplementary}.
Two additional reviewers participate in independent assessment and calibration reconciliation.
Dual screening of the full inclusion corpus was not possible given the scope and is acknowledged as a limitation per PRISMA 2020 item~16b~\cite{page2021prisma}.

The eleven included systems are OpenQASM~3.0, QIR, Q-MLIR, Qiskit, PennyLane, Q\#, Silq, Qmod, Quff, Qwerty and Qutes.
Tower fails C\textsubscript{2} and C\textsubscript{4} and is retained only as a labelled system of interest in Section~\ref{sec:system_descriptions}.

\subsection{Study quality assessment}\label{subsec:methodology:quality}
Evidence quality was assessed before extraction using a source hierarchy adapted from the evidence-based software engineering guidelines~\cite{KitchenhamCharters2007SLR}.
Source tier~1 comprises peer-reviewed journal or conference publications.
Source tier~2 comprises preprints from established research groups with a peer-reviewed publication programme.
Source tier~3 comprises official specifications, standards-body publications and maintained documentation.
No included system depends primarily on source tier~4 grey literature.

QIR and Q-MLIR use source tier~3 specification documentation as their primary and most authoritative source by design.
Not every IR or low-level framework has a peer-reviewed primary publication.
For QIR, this absence reflects the artefact's nature rather than incomplete retrieval.

Table~\ref{tab:quality_assessment} records primary citations, source tiers, confidence and leave-one-out flags.
\textit{High} confidence denotes a source tier~1 primary citation with at least two independent corroborating sources.
\textit{Medium} confidence denotes a source tier~2 primary citation or a system with one corroborating source.

\subsection{Data extraction and synthesis}\label{subsec:methodology:extraction}
For each system and task $T_1$--$T_4$, outcomes were recorded for conditions $C_1$--$C_9$ using the labels defined in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation_instrument}.
Each outcome is supported by a minimal code construction, primary-source citations and a one-sentence justification.

Canonical examples from primary papers, specifications, official documentation and official tutorials take precedence.
Constructed examples are used only where no canonical example exists for the task or feature.
They are explicitly labelled, with primary-source justification for every non-trivial feature.
Abridgement is permitted only where the omitted portion is a cited routine or referenced helper that is not the subject of the evidence claim.
It must not conceal the mechanism being assessed, particularly result extraction, composition or uncomputation.

Each system $\times$ task $\times$ condition record captures example type, source links, minimal code, backend/simulator/version preconditions, the outcome and justification, and required escape hatches or cross-layer leakage.
Absent or ambiguous evidence received the more conservative outcome label.
Outcomes inferred from examples marked optional or advanced were not used.
Appendix~C publishes the extraction dataset and Appendix~D the assessed code samples~\cite{kavadias2026qplSurveySupplementary}.

Calibration was conducted by two reviewers independently assessing three to five systems before proceeding to full independent assessment.
Disagreements greater than one level were discussed and resolved with a documented rationale; any amendments made post-calibration were applied prospectively.

Synthesis follows a structured narrative approach without meta-analysis~\cite{KitchenhamCharters2007SLR}.
Systems are grouped by taxonomy tier.
The aggregation rules in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation_instrument} produce a tier assignment justified by discriminating conditions, a compact condition profile and representative evidence bullets with citations.
Each system's synthesis identifies key evidence that is constructed rather than canonical.

\subsection{Sensitivity analysis and threats to validity}\label{subsec:methodology:sensitivity}
A pre-specified leave-one-out sensitivity analysis examined systems where one document provides the sole or pivotal evidence for tasks or conditions~\cite{kavadias2026qplSurveyProtocol}.
Two dependencies were identified.

\textit{Quff}: all outcomes depend on one peer-reviewed source~\cite{wright2024quff}.
No additional published specification or paper existed at the cutoff, so removing this source would leave no evidence base.
Outcomes are retained given the peer-reviewed venue, with the dependency acknowledged.

\textit{Qutes}: two tier-relevant outcomes depend critically on a preprint describing reversible lifetime semantics~\cite{reversiblelifetime2026}.
The HPDC~2025 paper alone would not sustain the same tier justification~\cite{faro2025qutes}.
The broader programme has peer-reviewed publications, but this mechanism's preprint status remains a limitation.

The principal threats to validity and mitigations are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Assessment subjectivity}: dual independent assessment, calibration, required evidence quotes and predefined rules for ambiguous cases.
    \item \textit{Task-suite dependence}: $T_1$--$T_4$ were pre-specified in the protocol; post-calibration amendments are documented and applied prospectively only~\cite{kavadias2026qplSurveyProtocol}.
    \item \textit{Ecosystem maturity}: source-tier labels and per-claim confidence ratings mitigate misattribution.
    \item \textit{Version churn}: the latest stable major release by 2026-04-08 is assessed consistently.
    \item \textit{Publication bias}: official specifications, standards-body publications and maintainer documentation are included to avoid under-representing IR-tier and industry-produced frameworks~\cite{KitchenhamCharters2007SLR,page2021prisma}.
    \item \textit{Evidence availability and representativeness}: uneven canonical coverage necessitates constructed examples, which can over-approximate idiomatic workflows.
    Constructed examples are labelled, each non-trivial feature requires primary-source justification and escape hatches or cross-layer leakage are recorded.
    \item \textit{Interpretation drift}: common condition ladders, shared-subset calibration, one-sentence outcome justifications and conservative treatment of ambiguous evidence mitigate inconsistent interpretation.
\end{itemize}
