\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Quantum-implicit entry criteria can be derived from the existing literature.
The eleven assessed systems populate the Assembly, IR, Framework and High-level tiers, but none meets the quantum-implicit requirements across the representative task suite (RQ1).
The persistent gap across the surveyed approaches is structural rather than merely a matter of missing features, justifying a new language targeting this tier (RQ2).

\paragraph{Limitations}
The theoretically grounded instrument has not undergone controlled validation of its condition definitions or the stability of F/C/P judgements across evaluators~\cite{Coblenz2021PLIERS,Myers2016PLUsabilitySIG}.
The findings reflect the field as of 2026-04-08 and do not account for subsequent system developments.
Practitioner evidence discussed in Section~\ref{sec:background} is observational, and a complementary user study would strengthen the design implications independently of instrument validation.
The pre-specified system selection underrepresents photonic computing languages and topological qubit frameworks, limiting generalisation across quantum computing paradigms.
The small, canonically grounded task suite may produce comparative patterns driven by what the examples and available primary-source evidence exercise rather than by systems' full capabilities.

\paragraph{Instrument validation}
Documentation-only learnability studies using T1--T4 and public documentation would provide observable proxies for required-knowledge pressure and related burden~\cite{Coblenz2021PLIERS}.
Formal inter-rater reliability studies would measure agreement on F/C/P outcomes across independent reviewers~\cite{Myers2016PLUsabilitySIG}.

\paragraph{System set and task suite expansion}
An expanded review could reconsider excluded systems and include systems that post-date protocol registration.
Domain-specific tasks covering quantum chemistry, variational algorithms and quantum networking would test the instrument beyond T1--T4~\cite{ferreira2024qplusage}.
OpenQASM's progression toward intermediate representation features also motivates revisiting tier boundaries as task suites exercise different capabilities.

\paragraph{Prototype language design}
The design implications in Section~\ref{subsec:discussion:implications} motivate a prototype that is quantum-implicit by default while retaining expert access for optimisation and hardware-specific tuning.
Its design and implementation should be evaluated against the gating constraints in Section~\ref{subsec:evaluation_instrument:gating} across the representative task suite, using the same evidence discipline as this review.
