\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{RQ1: Current abstraction levels and the quantum-implicit gap}\label{subsec:discussion:rq1}
No reviewed system meets the quantum-implicit requirements across the representative task suite, as recorded in Table~\ref{tab:system_assessments:system_summary}.
The recurring constraints concern primary artefacts, qubit identity and observation boundaries.
Their persistence gives the quantum-bias framing in Section~\ref{subsec:introduction:quantum_bias} a task-based evidential basis.

Assembly and IR systems provide instruction fidelity, interchange and compiler-facing representations, while Framework systems provide library abstraction, transpilation and execution infrastructure.
These systems address requirements within their intended roles rather than failing because they retain the representations they were designed to support.
The remaining vocabulary requirement is consistent with accounts of abstraction progress dominated by structure and tooling over circuit substrates~\cite{corralesgarro2025productivityexpressiveness,furntratt2024higherabstractionlevels}.
High-level systems simplify additional obligations but only partially meet the programmer-facing requirements identified in the literature~\cite{DiMatteo2024AbstractionHierarchy,nunez_corrales2025betterabstractmachines}.

Tier assignments do not capture all differences in structured reversal, observation handling and composition.
Q\#, Quff, Qutes and Qwerty provide program-like organisation or alternative expression mechanisms, yet their recorded profiles remain Framework because other obligations persist.

\subsection{RQ2: Extension pathways and barriers}\label{subsec:discussion:rq2}
The constrained outcomes identify abstraction mechanisms to extend, not merely obligations that systems fail to remove.
Silq's typed values and automatic uncomputation move programming away from explicit wire management~\cite{bichsel2020silq}.
Qmod's numeric and structured quantum types similarly separate value-level operations from circuit synthesis, although explicit allocation remains~\cite{vax2025qmodexpressivehighlevelquantum}.
Their constrained identity outcomes and passing composition outcomes in Table~\ref{tab:system_assessments:system_summary} distinguish this progress from circuit-library reuse.
A concrete extension would retain value-level composition while transferring allocation and lifetime decisions behind those interfaces, rather than requiring programmers to recover the underlying qubit representation.

Entanglement need not be exposed solely through programmer-managed resources.
Qutes' reversible-lifetime approach uses conservative dependency, liveness and entanglement analysis to determine safe reclamation points~\cite{reversiblelifetime2026}.
Together with Silq's type-directed safety, this suggests a pathway through compiler-maintained resource and correlation information.
Such mechanisms could support abstract values whose safe use and disposal are checked without requiring programmers to track their constituent wires.
The unresolved issue is whether these guarantees can be maintained across composed routines without exposing quantum-specific annotations or manual recovery obligations.
The existing analyses provide mechanisms for that investigation, not evidence that unrestricted inference is possible.

Task-local results show that this separation is achievable in some constructions.
Qutes' membership operation removes all assessed obligations, while Qwerty's Deutsch--Jozsa and Grover constructions pass the artefact and identity conditions.
Their remaining observation and task-dependent constraints show why removing wires is insufficient.
Extending this relief would require compositional result interfaces that manage extraction, alongside authoring abstractions that avoid reintroducing phase and entanglement reasoning.
The phase-estimation findings in Section~\ref{subsec:system_assessments:knowledge} identify where that broader contract remains unfulfilled; constructed examples limit the inference to the assessed workflows.

Quantum constraints must be respected by implementations, but do not prescribe their visibility to programmers~\cite{yuan2025foundationalabstractions}.
Tower illustrates an alternative representational starting point without establishing quantum-implicitness under this instrument.
The evidence therefore supports borrowing and extending existing value, safety and lifetime mechanisms while identifying the boundaries those extensions must cross.
A new language integrating these responsibilities is a justified design direction, not an established necessity; whether adaptation can achieve the same separation remains an implementation and evaluation question.

\subsection{Methodological reflections}\label{subsec:discussion:methodological_reflections}
OpenQASM's C7 passes despite the expected Assembly profile because the examples do not require timing, topology or pulse-level calibration.
Task under-coverage, examples that avoid hardware-control features or changes in default assembly workflows could explain this outcome.
The recorded pattern does not distinguish those explanations.

QIR's C6 lacks task-aligned observation evidence.
A missing outcome can reflect an unexercised capability, an obligation absent from the artefact or incomplete source coverage.
It must not be interpreted as either satisfied abstraction or demonstrated lack of capability.

\subsection{Design implications}\label{subsec:discussion:implications}
The findings motivate four requirements for either extension or a new language, consistent with the abstract-machine concerns identified in the literature.
Programs should be authored over abstract values rather than circuit objects, with resource identity and lifetime managed behind those interfaces.
Result extraction should likewise be governed by language and runtime contracts rather than programmer-selected measurement operations.
These separations must hold across composed routines, not only within specialised operations.

The default programming path should permit programs to be reasoned about without requiring amplitude, phase or entanglement vocabulary.
Explicit opt-ins should preserve expert control without making that expertise a prerequisite for the common path.
